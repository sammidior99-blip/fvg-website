Frequently asked questions

International curricula

What is the difference between IB, A-Level and AP?

The IB Diploma is a broad two-year programme with six subjects plus a core of Theory of Knowledge, the Extended Essay and CAS. A-Levels let students specialise in a smaller number of subjects, usually three or four. AP courses are individual university-level courses with exams, often taken alongside a school diploma. The best fit depends on the student's strengths and goals.

Does the curriculum affect which universities a student can apply to?

Most major international curricula are widely recognised, but specific courses may require particular subjects or grades. We check requirements for the universities and courses a student is interested in before key subject choices are made.

Academic planning

When should we start planning?

Earlier planning gives more options — especially for curriculum and subject choices — but it is never too late to get support. We tailor the plan to wherever the student is today.

Do you support graduate school applications?

Yes, including programme selection, building a research background, research statements (SOP) and CVs, interview preparation and scholarship applications.

Can you guarantee an admission result?

No, and we will not make that promise. Admission decisions are made by universities. We commit to planning, mentoring, progress management and academic training that we can deliver, so that students apply in the strongest position they can. We also do not promise specific recommendation letters or publications.

What happens in the first consultation?

We learn about the student's grades, interests and goals, answer your questions and outline realistic options. It helps to bring recent school reports, a list of activities and your questions. Afterwards you receive initial recommendations and a suggested next step.

OUR JUDGEMENT

Admission was never decided by grades alone.

Judgement first, then the path, then delivery. This industry is used to sending in documents first; we work out the path first. What really decides an application is which pool a student competes in, who evaluates them, and how they are judged.

JUDGEMENT 01

Hard numbers get you in the door; depth decides your place.

School, GPA and test scores set the threshold. Essays, referees and research depth decide the ranking above it. Four years of consistent academic work weighs more than a score raised at the last minute.

JUDGEMENT 02

A difference in school status is a difference in applicant pool.

With the same grades, students on different school tracks face clearly different odds. It is not that the student is not good enough; they are running in a more crowded lane.

JUDGEMENT 03

Building a profile is not stacking activities.

Admissions officers do not look at the length of an activity list, but at a sustained, deepening record in one field and the depth of thinking behind it.

SIX VARIABLES

Planning starts by taking the application apart

An overseas application is made of six things. They interlock, and any one handled in isolation gets recalculated in application season.

01

School track & diploma

The credibility behind a transcript; it decides how the whole file is read.

02

Courses & grades

Four years of consistent academic work is the admissions officer's first filter.

03

Standardised tests

The ticket to the shortlist. Reach the bar and stop; beyond it, depth matters more.

04

Profile & ability

The strongest differentiator. Verifiable academic depth beats a list of activities.

05

Recommendations

Third-party confirmation, written by people who actually taught or supervised the student.

06

Application & admission

Presentation and summary. The first five set the ceiling for this one.

No single one of the six matters most. What makes the difference is how well they fit together. Whichever one slips first is where application season will recalculate.
